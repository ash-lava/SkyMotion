AVAILABILITY AND SCIENCE LIMITS
IRSA 502 errors remain intermittent and are not treated as empty results.
A real M101 October–December query succeeded with an empty table; WISE's
November query succeeded with records and enforced the requested date range.
Gaia's live optional overlay request returned 502. Full 102-channel spectra,
calibrated light curves, PSF-matched subtraction, uncertainty propagation,
weighted multi-image coadds and color movies are not implemented. Flagged
finite pixels remain; flags include status bits and are not silently called
contamination. Archive samples and wavelength matches can be incomplete.

DISPLAY AND FILTER
The PNG is RGBA. Non-finite science pixels have alpha 0; valid pixels,
including valid zeros, have alpha 255. The existing grayscale stretch and
north-up orientation are retained. A CSS hatch behind the image is visible
only through the transparent pixels, with an explanatory legend.

The optional At least 95% covered selector checks actual frame-info/FITS
coverage for the loaded sequence, sequentially, with progress and cancellation.
Playback pauses during checks. Only verified frames meeting the threshold
enter filtered playback. Unverified frames are reported and excluded from
that view, rather than being called zero coverage. All frames restores the
original sequence; changing the target/detector/search cancels old checks.

Display positions map to original source indices for images, metadata and
Gaia overlays. Export retains all returned rows with measured coverage where
known, displayed flags, displayed source indices and the chosen threshold.
The filter does not reject quality flags. Brightness-proxy measurements keep
the original sequence and explicitly disclose that when filtering is active.

INFRARED COLOR FEASIBILITY
Fresh detector-filtered SIA queries for M101 returned genuine D1, D3 and D6
records. Actual IRSA FITS cutouts were downloaded and aligned on the same
6-arcminute, 192 x 192 sky grid. The primary celestial WCS is TAN-SIP; the
alternate spectral WCS ignores SIP as in the existing image pipeline.

The documented static RGB preview assigns:
- Blue:  D1, center wavelength 0.9905724551 um, 22 May 2025 16:02:50 UTC.
- Green: D3, center wavelength 2.1955285846 um, 9 May 2025 03:33:55 UTC.
- Red:   D6, center wavelength 4.8509102622 um, 9 May 2025 03:33:55 UTC.

Only pixels valid in all three channels are shown; common coverage is
0.9989149305555556. Each channel uses an independent 5-99.5 percentile
square-root stretch. The preview is explicitly labeled with its mixed dates.
docs/spherex-color-provenance.json records sources, dates, wavelengths,
coverage and download hashes. tools/make_color_example.py regenerates the
example from these documented public IRSA cutouts. Fresh regeneration was
checked against the original RGB/alpha data and reproduced its pixel values.

This is a static feasibility example, not simultaneous color or a color
playback mode. The date span is about 13.5 days. Motion, variability, flags,
PSF and processing differences can cause colored features; the independently
stretched colors are qualitative rather than calibrated spectral ratios.
The application does not use the saved example as a runtime data fallback.

LIMITS AND DEPLOYMENT
Validated locally against IRSA, not after deploying to Render. Coverage is
measured from finite aligned science pixels, not an instrument quality-mask
policy. The >=95% filter applies only to the loaded sample, can reduce the
time baseline and can take a minute or longer on its first run. It cannot
fill a detector gap or guarantee identical coverage at every sky position.
External service failures remain possible. No coaddition or synthetic sky
is introduced, and the archive search still reports limited live samples.
